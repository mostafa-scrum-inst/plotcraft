### Findings

**1. CD-1 is exceeded on small narrow footprints (owner FS, with QA / TL / PM; fix now, before F3-T1's sweep is fixed).**
CD-1 says "up to 3:1". In round 4 I accepted "3:1 plus less than one 0.5 m rounding step", and that is what the PM's examples show (6 x 18.5 m). The increment-1 cap that was built, `long <= 3 x (short + 0.5 m)` (A-6 step 8), allows a full extra step **per 0.5 m of the short side's rounding, times 3**, i.e. up to 1.5 m more.
- 4b's own 3:1 and 1:3 tries round width and depth down independently, so they reach that range.
- In my sweep, 46 of 2 768 footprints are longer than `3 x short + 0.5 m`. Examples:
  - 2.5 x 8.5 m (3.4 : 1), 8.5 x 30 m plot, 10 %, setbacks 5 / 2 / 3;
  - 3.5 x 11.5 m (3.3 : 1), 7.5 x 30 m plot, 20 %;
  - 4 x 13 m, 8 x 30 m plot, 25 %;
  - 7 x 22 m, 9 x 30 m plot, 60 %, setbacks 1 m.
- These are real results the client would see, they contradict his binding decision, and they widen the range F3-T1's generator must be proven on (the TL just set it to `3 x (short + 0.5)`, e.g. 4 x 13.5 m).

Fix:
- For the 4b candidates only, cap the long side at `3 x short + 0.5 m` after rounding (shortened about the same centre, as step 8 does now).
- 2:1 results keep today's cap, so F2-T1 results, the sample and every PM / CTO example (108, 75, 392, 90, 99, 111 m2) stay byte-identical. In my sweep, every footprint not affected by this is already within one step.
- Then F3-T1's sweep range becomes "3:1 plus one 0.5 m step", e.g. 6 x 18.5 and 4 x 12.5 m.

This is a few lines in `site-footprint.js` plus a unit case (2.5 x 8.5 -> 2.5 x 8.0 on that plot), QA's `f2-t1-ref.mjs` cap, A-6 step 8, and the 3.1 / 4.3 sentence ("long side at most 3 x short side + 0.5 m when stretched").

**2. Architecture adherence and hard constraints: followed.**
- Single file, classic scripts on one `PC`, `localStorage` only in `ui/storage.js` (all draft keys now go through `readDraft`, `writeDraft` and `removeDraft`), no native dialogs; lint 0.
- Core stays pure (vm loader with no globals; the lint core-purity rule). The largest JS file is 251 lines (`stage-plot.js`).
- Every layout-class control goes through `propose`, and every appearance control through `commit`. I checked every binding in `stage-questions.js` and `q-rooms.js`: Q2, Q3, Q6, Q7 (gate or own dialog), Q11–Q14 and edge toggles are gated; Q1, Q4, Q5, Q8–Q10 and Q15–Q17 commit.

**3. Design quality: better than planned in two places.**
- **Override clearing.** It is one normaliser (`PC.site.clearOvIfOutlineChanged`) run by `commit` and on the gate candidate, instead of "every mutator clears", which A-4 originally asked for. A future mutator therefore cannot forget it. My probe found no way to leave an override on an open plot, a moved outline or a changed street edge.
- **Own-confirmation dialogs.** The Q7 removal and the floor-plan sentences are computed from `PC.store.affected` on the exact candidate of each choice, so the dialog cannot disagree with the gate.
- **Room-list rules** live as pure, refusing helpers in `PC.levels`, which return `{ ok, reason }` and leave the draft untouched. The UI tries them on a clone and only then proposes.
- **Two weaknesses, both accepted:**
  - `levels.js` now mixes rules with dialog wording (`removalQuestion`, `plansSentence`); `validate.js` already sets that pattern of texts in core.
  - `q-rooms.attempt` runs the helper twice (clone, then in the gate). That is harmless because the helpers refuse without side effects.

- Rule for the next bump (F3 or F4 will add fields only if needed; plans already exist in v1 / v2):
  - add one `migrate` step per version, chained (v1 -> v2 -> v3);
  - add the old key to `KEYS`;
